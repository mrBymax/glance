## Punchline v2

Glance tells, nod buys: a protocol for paying with your glasses. In a store, grab what you need and skip the queue. Your glasses see what you pick up, and a nod pays for the whole cart.

# Description

Pick up an oat drink and look at it. Your glasses say: "Oat drink, one litre, one eighty-nine." Nod. "Added oat drink."

At the self-checkout, look at the code on the screen and nod once more. Paid. Exit.

No scanner, no phone, no touchscreen, no queue. It runs today on real Ray-Ban Meta glasses.

Meta Glance is a protocol for paying anything hands-free: look at something to see what it costs, then nod to buy it. And you’re done. The protocol extends to all products, from stores to public transportation; today we present Glance for stores: install the app, or come and try Glance for stores at our table!

## Glance for stores

Our initial use-case focuses on usage within stores, and is ready to deploy out of the box.
- **Full hands.** Scan-as-you-shop already runs in 67 S Group stores in Finland, and it still costs you a hand. Glance gives it back.
- **Blind and low-vision shoppers.** 83% of visually impaired shoppers cannot easily access packaging information (GS1 UK), and a self-checkout is a flat touchscreen. Glance reads the label aloud and replaces the touchscreen with a nod.
- **No refit.** Other approaches, such as ceiling cameras, needed a refit of every store, and Amazon removed them from its Fresh stores in 2024. Glance puts the camera on the shopper.
- **Quick adoption.** Glance fits the store as it is, with no new hardware. The store gives Meta access to its list of products and prices, and its checkout screens show one code. If the store has exit barriers, your glasses send a barcode to your phone after you pay, and it opens them.
- **Glance tells, nod buys.** Looking never changes your cart. A nod counts only for the product you just heard, while it is still in view.
- **No guessing.** Prices and allergen warnings come from the store catalog. For look-alike products the voice asks you to scan the product barcode.
- **Works for every ability.** Every action also works by voice, touchpad or a phone button, and every spoken line is captioned.

### Under the hood

The store prototype is built on the Meta Wearables Device Access Toolkit with Gemini recognition. Glance recovers from a lost connection, a paused session and a low battery. If a fault cannot be recovered, it fails safely: no exit barcode is issued, the barrier stays closed, and you pay at a regular checkout with your cart still on your phone.

## Glance beyond stores

The protocol is not tied to shopping. Any service with a price can use it. Imagine a bus company, they would only need to add a Meta Glance sticker to let you pay with your glasses. The same process can open a gate, pay a vending machine or a parking meter. In the future, the same glance and nod can send money to a friend.

Our vision is a world where you leave your phone at home, and you do everything in a glance.

**Page 3.** Two photos side by side: a queue at a checkout belt, and a shopper with a basket in one hand and a phone in the other. Titles: "Queue and unload" and "One hand short".

## 2:25 The protocol (75 s)

**Page 10.** Four numbered steps in a row: "1 Look. 2 Hear. 3 Nod. 4 Done."

Look again at what happened at that checkout. A place showed a code. The glasses read it. A voice said what a nod would do. A nod confirmed, and the place got the answer.

Look, hear, nod, done. Not one of those steps is about groceries.

**Page 11.** A bus door with a Glance code sticker next to it. Caption: "Line 4 to the harbor. Single ticket. Nod to buy."

This is a Glance code. It is a QR code. It goes on a screen, or on a sticker.

Put it by the door of a bus. You board, you look at it, and you hear: "Line 4 to the harbor. Single ticket. Nod to buy." You nod and you sit down. No app to open, no card to tap.

**Page 12.** Four tiles with an icon each: "Ticket gate", "Vending machine", "Parking meter", "Restaurant table". Line at the bottom: "Any place that can show a code can take a nod."

Put it on a ticket gate, and a nod opens the gate. Put it on a vending machine or a parking meter, and a nod pays. Put it on a restaurant table, and it reads you the menu.

Any place that can show a code can take a nod. The place registers with the Glance server and gets the result when you nod. That is the whole integration.

This is a vision that we have, where you can leave the phone at home.
